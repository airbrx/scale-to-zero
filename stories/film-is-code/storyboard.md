# The Film Is the Code: the storyboard

The plan for `story.js` and the score, beat by beat, against `script.md`.

**The idea.** A video file is a crate shipped whole on every play. Our
film is a letter of instructions, and the reader's browser folds the film
out of it on their own table. The turn is the crate itself unfolding flat:
it was paper all along.

## The four questions

**The place.** A street of houses folded out of the page, with a depot at
the end of the road (the CDN). Each house is a reader.
- The depot sends a lorry with a crate the size of a room for every play.
  The crate is the video file. Five smaller crates sit in its yard: the
  rendition ladder.
- Behind the street, in the platform beat, three towers (Vimeo, Gfycat,
  YouTube) with ropes to the houses' roofs. Coupling is a rope.
- In the rebuild, a paper bot walks the same road with an envelope and a
  small reel (the voice). The reader's house opens like a doll's house; on
  the table inside, the sheet unfolds into a tiny street. The film is our
  street, folded on their table.

**What we follow.** One play, at the reader's house (the middle one): the
click, the window lighting, the delivery.

**The spine.** Two receipts at the right edge: *video* in red, *drawn* in
ink. Video's grows with every lorry and jumps at a thousand plays; drawn
barely moves.

**The rhyme.** The same street, the same click, the same camera move,
rewound. An envelope instead of a lorry. The depot cools, and at {idle}
folds flat.

**The numbers live on the phone**, each with its source: $0.085/GB, 83 MB,
83 GB, $7, $200 and $3,500, $1.38B, 1.3 MB and 32 requests, ~150 KB, 1.1 MB,
1.3 GB, $0.11, 250 KB, 0 frames paused, 0 MB unpacked. The cold open's two weights sit
under their objects; that's the receipt.

**The ends.** It opens on a crate and an envelope dropped on the page: 83
MB and 1.3 MB, the same film. It closes on the reader's table, the tiny street
still standing, then everything folding back into the page.
